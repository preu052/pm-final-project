# Hypothesis & Success Metrics (Module 3)

## Vorarbeit · Hypothesenprüfung
- **Role , who you are solving for (from M2):** A parent or self-paying customer who is financially responsible for a service and needs to understand exactly what the installment agreement covers.
- **Goal , what this user is ultimately trying to achieve:** Understand one clear and predictable payment commitment—how much is financed, what the monthly payment is, and how that obligation may change over time.
- **Friction / moment of misery , the specific pain blocking their goal:** The financing journey becomes confusing when the real-world situation does not match the product's assumptions: the payer may not be the service recipient, the final amount may still change, or it may be unclear whether the plan covers the total treatment cost, a single invoice, or only a co-payment. The customer is forced to make a financial decision without a clear picture of what they are actually committing to.
- **Current workaround , the external tool or manual process they rely on (M2):** 1. Collect the treatment plan, invoice or cost estimate. 2. Compare it with the installment offer to understand what amount is actually financed. 3. Clarify who must sign/pay if payer and service recipient differ. 4. Contact the partner when amounts, reimbursements or future costs are unclear. 5. Recalculate the expected monthly commitment whenever the underlying cost changes.
- **Problem Hook , your one-sentence framing of the business crisis (M1):** We must prevent increasing product fragmentation from limiting the scalability of BFS's installment-plan business by replacing repeated client-specific configurations with a standardized, configurable installment-plan framework that dynamically applies the right rules for each customer, transaction, client, and market.
- **Value Proposition , the outcome your initiative promised to deliver (M1):** For BFS clients that need flexible installment solutions across different customer groups, markets, and use cases, we will provide a standardized but configurable installment-plan framework that applies reusable rules while allowing controlled variation where needed. This creates faster onboarding, easier adaptation to market and regulatory requirements, and less dependency on bespoke implementations. It is urgent because the current project-based approach limits BFS’s ability to scale efficiently into new verticals while complexity continues to grow.

## Schritt 3 · Formuliere deine Hypothese
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** The financing journey becomes confusing when the real-world situation does not match the product's assumptions: the payer may not be the service recipient, the final amount may still change, or it may be unclear whether the plan covers the total treatment cost, a single invoice, or only a co-payment. The customer is forced to make a financial decision without a clear picture of what they are actually committing to.
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** 46.2K of 316K installment agreements have a manual contact attempt after creation, around 14.6%. These cases show stronger signs of payment or adjustment needs: 34.35% direct debit return rate vs. 13.00% overall, 14.99% extension rate vs. 6.76% overall, plus 6.05K extended and 6.73K shortened agreements. In orthodontics, 920 of 7.12K agreements, around 12.9%, have post-creation contact, so KFO is not more contact-heavy than the overall base. The issue appears broader than one specialty and more linked to post-setup management needs.
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** A parent or self-paying customer who is financially responsible for a service and needs to understand exactly what the installment agreement covers.
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** The Responsible Payer cannot make an informed installment decision when it is unclear who is responsible for payment, what amount is covered, or whether the plan applies to the total cost, one invoice, or only a co-payment.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** Customers choose and complete an installment agreement with a clear understanding of their commitment. This should reduce avoidable follow-up and adjustments, support agreement retention, and reduce preventable drop-off that could affect revenue or contribute to churn.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** A 20% relative reduction in the share of agreements with a post-creation manual contact tagged as a question about the payer, covered amount, or plan scope.
The current overall manual-contact rate is 14.6%, but it includes different reasons for contact. Tagging contact reasons will establish the relevant baseline.
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** The overall direct debit return rate and extension rate must not worsen by more than 2 percentage points compared with the baseline or a comparable control group.
Current overall reference rates are 13.00% for direct debit returns and 6.76% for extensions.
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** Run an 8–12 week pilot, with at least 300 eligible agreements before making a scale decision. First ensure that post-creation contacts can be tagged by reason. Scale if clarification-related contacts fall by at least 20% and the guardrails remain within limits; otherwise, review the findings and decide whether to pivot or stop.
- **Draft your full hypothesis sentence , one to three sentences; quote the metric, name the persona, name the outcome:** Based on qualitative evidence that customers may be unsure who pays and what the installment plan covers, and quantitative evidence that 46.2K of 316K agreements (14.6%) have a post-creation manual contact, I believe that making the payer, covered amount, and plan scope clear for the Responsible Payer will reduce post-creation clarification contacts by 20% relative to baseline. I will protect the overall direct debit return rate (13.00%) and extension rate (6.76%), and make a go/no-go decision after 8–12 weeks and at least 300 eligible agreements.
