Slide 5 · Strategy, problem, value & hypothesis

Make the payment change
clear and manageable.

One focused problem
Problem hook

When a responsible payer needs a different collection date, they cannot change it in the portal or see which installment will be affected. They have to contact Support.

Value proposition

The portal lets eligible payers choose an allowed collection date and see when the change takes effect. This gives them more control and can replace a manual support request.

Data-backed hypothesis

Based on M3 showing 46.2K of 316K agreements (14.6%) with a post-creation manual contact, I believe that letting eligible Responsible Payers change their collection date in the portal will reduce collection-date-related manual contacts by 20% relative within 30 days. I will protect the direct-debit return rate and make a go/no-go decision after the required sample and two full collection cycles.

14.6% includes all contact reasons. Establish the collection-date-specific baseline before interpreting results.

B2 · PRD must-haves
  • Show current collection date and eligible dates.
  • Show which installment will use the new date before confirmation.
  • Show success only after the change is saved.
  • Keep installment amount and agreement term unchanged.
Scope boundaries

Should: Explain unavailable dates and suggest a next step. Track starts, completions, and failures through B10.

Won’t now: Manage multiple agreements, set a preferred date, add a calendar, change amounts or terms, defer payment, update bank details, or replace core systems.

Slide 8 · Validation · experiment plan

Test the change
before scaling it.

50 / 50 A/B
Hypothesis under test

Letting eligible payers change their collection date in the portal and see when it takes effect will reduce collection-date-related manual contacts within 30 days.

Control A

Payers can view the agreement in the portal but must contact Support to request a date change.

Variant B · one change

Add the date-change flow: current date, allowed dates, affected installment, and confirmation after save.

Ship

Iterate

Stop

Safety or reliability issue

Pause for a breached guardrail or any confirmed incorrect collection date.

Final readout only after the required sample, 30-day contact window, and at least two full collection cycles for both groups. Keep eligibility, date rules, amounts, terms, payment process, notifications, and Support service constant.

Slide 10 · Story · individual insights

The key was separating
the broad problem from B2.

Reflection
Friction points

The broader customer problem is uncertainty about who pays and what the plan covers. B2, however, changes when a payment is collected.

Aha moment

The 14.6% contact rate includes all reasons. It cannot prove that collection-date questions are common or that B2 will reduce them. The test needs date-specific contact tagging and a matching baseline.

Key learnings
  • Use evidence that matches the feature being tested.
  • Separate the customer action, the service outcome, and the payment guardrail.
  • Keep experiment exposure to one customer-facing change.
What I’d do next

Establish the collection-date-specific baseline, recalculate the sample, validate the flow with eligible payers, and run the controlled pilot. Scale only if tagged contacts fall and payment reliability holds.

Make the claim no broader than the evidence.07 / 08
